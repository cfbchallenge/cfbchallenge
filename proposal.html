01 — The problem

Fix the incentives that are hollowing out the regular season.

A 24-team playoff gives more programs a chance to compete for a championship. But a larger postseason also risks making individual regular-season games less consequential.

Rather than simply expanding the playoff, college football should build a system that gives teams more reasons to win meaningful games throughout the year.

Bring back marquee matchups

Replace the incentive to avoid elite opponents with nationally coordinated nonconference showdowns.

Make losses survivable

A competitive loss against an excellent team shouldn't be more damaging than avoiding that team entirely.

Restore conference rivalries

Stable divisions create recognizable conference races and protect important annual games.

Earn playoff access

Qualification games replace committee decisions for most of the national playoff field.

The goal isn't simply more football. It's more football that matters.
02 — Conference structure

Restore divisions and create meaningful conference races.

The proposal does not require another round of realignment. Instead, each major conference is organized into two stable, geographically sensible divisions.

Two divisions

Smaller races create clearer standings, more familiar opponents and meaningful late-season competition.

Eight conference games

Each major-conference team plays eight league games while preserving four nonconference dates.

Four nonconference opportunities

Challenge weekends, Group of Six games and open dates fit into a coordinated national schedule.

Standings determine opportunity

Division finishes establish Play-In Weekend pairings, with postseason qualification decided on the field.

Conference play earns access. Nonconference play helps determine national ranking, seeding and home-field advantage.

03 — Conference Challenges and Draft Days

Turn the nonconference schedule into a national event.

Instead of scheduling marquee games a decade in advance, commissioners build their Challenge slates each spring through live, televised drafts.

Four spring draft events

Conference matchups are revealed through major television events featuring coaches, returning stars and commissioner strategy.

Current teams, current matchups

Spring selections produce games that reflect the teams fans will actually see that fall.

Standard Bearers

Each conference designates a Standard Bearer. The Big Ten and SEC representatives meet in the featured Commissioners' Classic.

Conference-wide stakes

Challenge results determine postseason berth allocations, giving every matchup consequences beyond the individual teams.

The flagship events

The Big Ten–SEC Challenge owns Labor Day weekend, featuring 16 head-to-head games. If the conferences split 8–8, the Commissioners' Classic determines the Challenge winner.

The ACC–Big 12 Challenge follows in Week 3, with additional rotating cross-conference Challenges in Week 4.

The rotating matchups can also shift postseason berths. An ACC or Big 12 conference that wins at least 9–7 against its Big Ten or SEC opponent can take one of that conference's allocated berths.

04 — Objective national rankings

Replace the committee with a season-long climb.

Instead of weekly subjective rankings, teams accumulate points throughout the season. Every performance contributes to a running total that determines playoff rankings and seeding.

Past results are continuously reassessed as opponents win or lose. A victory over a team that develops into a contender becomes more valuable. A victory over a team that struggles can lose some of its original value.

Winning

Victories provide the foundation of the system. Beating elite opponents offers the largest rewards.

Game location

Neutral-site (+0.25) and road (+0.50) location multipliers recognize the added difficulty of winning away from home.

Game control

Using only the score throughout the game, the system recognizes dominant performances and competitive efforts, including some losses.

Strength of opponent

The one element incorporating outside power ratings. Opponent strength changes the value of past performances as the season develops.

A five-point loss at Ohio State can be more impressive than a 28-point victory over Rutgers. Six competitive losses won't build a playoff résumé, but one extra loss from scheduling a great opponent shouldn't derail a season.

Winning remains the most valuable outcome. The goal is to make defeating a great opponent enormously rewarding while allowing teams to survive competitive losses against excellent competition.

The exact formula would be finalized through testing, published in advance and applied equally to every team.

05 — The national championship field

24 teams. 17 automatic berths. Seven at-large places.

Most teams earn access by winning a postseason qualification game, not by being selected by a committee.

9Big Ten + SEC combined
5ACC + Big 12 combined
3Group of Six qualification winners
7At-large selections by objective rankings

Big Ten and SEC

The Challenge winner receives five automatic Play-In berths; the losing conference receives four.

ACC and Big 12

The Challenge winner receives three automatic Play-In berths; the losing conference receives two.

Group of Six

Six conference champions play three qualification games, sending three winners into the national bracket.

Seven at-large places

The objective national rankings fill the remaining positions and seed the complete 24-team field.

Rotating Challenges may transfer up to two berths between conferences without changing the total number of automatic qualifiers.

Nine major berths plus five major berths plus three Group of Six berths equals 17 automatic qualifiers every year.
06 — Play-In Weekend

Replace conference championship games with direct qualification games.

In Week 15, major-conference teams compete in cross-division matchups. Each winner earns an automatic playoff berth.

The number of available berths determines how deep into each division's standings the Play-In extends. A conference with five berths sends its top five teams from each division into five qualification games.

The best finisher from one division faces the lowest qualifying finisher from the other. Higher division placement earns a more favorable matchup, but nobody qualifies without winning.

Win your Play-In game, and you're in. No committee interpretation required.
07 — The national bracket

Reward the best regular seasons without removing the danger.

All 24 playoff teams are seeded using the objective rankings. Better regular-season performance provides meaningful advantages throughout the bracket.

Eight first-round byes

The top eight seeds skip the opening round, making elite ranking position a major prize.

Campus playoff games

Higher seeds host the first two national rounds, turning ranking position into real home-field advantage.

Fixed bracket

No reseeding. Teams and fans know the route to the championship as soon as the field is announced.

Geography matters

Early-round placement limits unnecessary travel where practical without overriding national seeding.

New Year's Day quarterfinals

The final eight teams take over the sport's traditional postseason holiday.

Saturday night championship

The season ends on a predictable weekend stage rather than drifting further into January.

09 — The complete vision

Meaningful games are not extra inventory. They are the product.

The CFB Challenge embraces a larger playoff while protecting what makes college football special: great rivalries, blockbuster games, conference pride and a regular season in which winning has real consequences.

Conference Challenges create premium matchups. Divisions create meaningful races. Objective rankings reward ambitious schedules. Play-In games determine most playoff qualifiers on the field.

Better schedules. Meaningful conference competition. A stronger postseason.
