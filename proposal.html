A new way forward for college football's schedule

The full CFB Challenge proposal

The BCS created an urgency and national interest unmatched by any other sport's regular season. The College Football Playoff eased some of that pressure, but it also gave more teams access and produced more unforgettable games. Now college football has entered an era in which continued playoff expansion feels inevitable as television partners seek more inventory.

The question is whether the sport simply adds more postseason games—or builds a system that also protects what makes college football worth watching. The CFB Challenge is a complete overhaul of nonconference scheduling, conference structure without expansion, national rankings and a 24-team playoff in which 17 teams play their way in on the field.

01

Why the sport needs a new system

Fix the incentives that are hollowing out the regular season

The current structure asks teams to protect their playoff résumé, survive oversized conferences and hope a committee values their schedule correctly. The CFB Challenge changes those incentives.

Bring back marquee nonconference games

Those games are disappearing because the risk increasingly outweighs the reward. The Challenge replaces isolated scheduling gambles with blockbuster games carrying conference-wide value.

A good loss no longer ends a season

Conference play determines whether a team can earn automatic access. Nonconference play determines how high that team can climb in the rankings and where it is seeded.

Restore conference rivalries

Modern conferences are too large for every important matchup to be protected. Stable divisions make the games fans care about part of the annual schedule again.

Make a playoff require playing in

College football calls it a playoff. Most teams should qualify by winning a game with a berth on the line—not by being selected in a private meeting room.

02

Conference structure and scheduling

Use divisions to create smaller conference races—without conference expansion

The CFB Challenge does not redraw the map or force another round of realignment. It organizes today's large conferences into divisions, restores repeat opponents and connects division standings directly to Play-In Weekend.

Two stable divisions

Each major conference is divided into two geographic groups, creating coherent races and a schedule fans can understand from year to year.

Eight conference games

Teams play a consistent conference slate that preserves divisional rivals while leaving four dates for the rebuilt nonconference system.

Four nonconference games

Open dates, Challenge games and Group of Six matchups fit into a common national structure instead of being scattered randomly across the season.

Standings lead somewhere

Division finish determines each team's Play-In pairing, so conference games decide postseason opportunity without a single conference championship rematch.

The regular season has two connected jobs: conference play earns access; nonconference play establishes national strength, ranking ceiling and home-field advantage.
03

Conference Challenges and spring Draft Days

Turn the nonconference schedule into a season of national events

The Challenge Draft is the centerpiece that can attract attention to the entire proposal: major spring television events create current matchups, then conference-wide Challenge weekends deliver blockbuster games with playoff berths attached.

Four nationally televised spring drafts

Shortly after spring practice, the participating conferences reveal their Challenge schedules through live events built as part selection show, part media day and part schedule release.

Every school helps create the event

Head coaches and returning stars attend, take the stage when matchups are announced and create immediate interviews, photos, reactions and months of debate.

Matchups are built for the teams that exist now

Commissioners create the slate in the spring instead of asking fans to care about games scheduled a decade before kickoff.

Standard Bearers headline the weekend

Each conference names a Standard Bearer. In the Big Ten–SEC Challenge, those teams meet in the Labor Day night SEC/B1G Commissioners' Classic.

Two annual flagship Challenges

The Big Ten–SEC Challenge owns Labor Day weekend. The ACC–Big 12 Challenge follows one week later, giving both conference pairings a yearly championship race.

A rotating second round

In 2026, the Big Ten faces the Big 12 while the SEC faces the ACC. In 2027, the pairings switch to Big Ten–ACC and SEC–Big 12.

The lower conferences can steal a berth

The ACC or Big 12 earns an additional guaranteed playoff place by winning its rotating Challenge against the Big Ten or SEC, 9–7 or better.

Every game belongs to one scoreboard

A middle-of-the-pack road upset counts beside the prime-time showcase. Conference bragging rights and postseason access are decided across the full slate.

Imagine the finish: the Big Ten leads the Big Ten–SEC Challenge 8–7 with only the 16th game remaining: Georgia at Ohio State in the Standard Bearer matchup. It is winner-take-all. An Ohio State win delivers the Big Ten a 9–7 Challenge victory. A Georgia win makes it 8–8 and gives the SEC the Challenge through the Standard Bearer tiebreaker. Conference bragging rights, the likely No. 1 ranking and the larger guaranteed playoff allotment all come down to Labor Day night.

04

Objective national rankings

Rank teams by who they beat, where they beat them and how they beat them

The rankings are not produced by subjective committee discussion behind closed doors. The method is published before the season, and the results play out in public on the field.

Who did you beat?

Opponent quality is the foundation. Wins rise or fall in value as the teams on the schedule establish their own strength.

Where did you beat them?

Road success carries more weight than winning the same game at home, giving difficult environments measurable value.

How did you beat them?

The system distinguishes dominant, controlled performances from narrow escapes without allowing reputation alone to define quality.

Conference play opens the door

A team can recover from a strong nonconference loss because its conference finish still controls the automatic path into Play-In Weekend.

Nonconference play sets the ceiling

Challenge wins and ambitious road games determine whether a qualifier is ranked No. 1, earns a bye or hosts playoff games.

Everything is visible

Weekly rankings move because of published results and known inputs—not because a committee changes its explanation from one week to the next.

05

The 24-team championship field

Seventeen automatic berths. Seven at-large places.

Fourteen major-conference Play-In winners and three Group of Six winners claim automatic places. The remaining seven teams enter through the objective national rankings.

Big Ten + SEC 9 berths The annual Challenge winner earns five Play-In places; the other conference earns four.
ACC + Big 12 5 berths The annual Challenge winner earns three Play-In places; the other conference earns two.
Group of Six 3 berths Six conference champions meet in three Week 15 qualification games.
At-large field 7 berths The objective rankings fill the remaining places and seed the complete bracket.
The total never changes

Nine plus five plus three equals 17 automatic berths every season. They are part of the field from the beginning—not late-added bonus bids.

Two berths can be stolen

The rotating Challenges let the ACC or Big 12 take one berth from its Big Ten or SEC opponent by winning at least 9–7, shifting access without expanding the field.

06

CFB Challenge Play-In Weekend

Replace conference championship games with direct qualification games

Conference standings determine who plays, Challenge results determine how many berths each conference has, and every Play-In winner enters the national bracket.

Berth allotment sets the depth

A conference with five berths sends the top five teams from each division into mirrored cross-division matchups. Four berths use the top four, three use the top three, and so on.

Division finish creates the matchup

The highest finisher in one division faces the lowest qualifier from the other, rewarding the regular season without giving anyone a free pass into the field.

November matters deeper into the standings

Teams in the bottom-middle of a division are still fighting to reach a Play-In position late in the season. Even a fifth-place team can earn a puncher's chance with one win.

Every place in the division matters

Moving from fifth to fourth—or fourth to third—changes the opponent waiting in Week 15, keeping races meaningful even after first place is out of reach.

Win and you are in

There is no committee interpretation attached to the result. Every major-conference Play-In winner claims one of the 14 automatic berths.

Group of Six champions get the same chance

The six champions play three national qualification games in Week 15, with all three winners advancing to the 24-team playoff.

07

The national bracket

Reward the best regular seasons without removing the danger

The objective rankings seed all 24 teams. Top seeds receive meaningful advantages, but every national champion still has to survive a fixed path on the field.

Eight first-round byes

The top eight teams skip the opening national round, making elite ranking position a major regular-season prize.

Campus playoff games

Higher seeds host the first two national rounds, turning résumé strength into real home-field advantage.

A fixed bracket

No reseeding. Teams and fans know the complete route to the championship as soon as the field is announced.

Geography matters early

Placement limits unnecessary travel when possible without overriding the integrity of the national seed line.

New Year's Day quarterfinals

The final eight teams take over the sport's traditional holiday in four national showcase games.

A Saturday night championship

The season concludes on a predictable weekend stage rather than drifting deeper into January.

08

The full 20-week season

A clear calendar from the opening weekend through the national title

Every part of the proposal has a defined place: open dates, four Challenge windows, conference play, Group of Six championships, Play-In Weekend and five weeks of national playoff football.

Week 1
National open week

Every team may take an early bye or play a regularly scheduled nonconference game.

Week 2 · Labor Day
Big Ten–SEC Challenge Weekend

The flagship Challenge fills Labor Day weekend. Other conferences are open, with room for Group of Six Challenge events.

Week 3
ACC–Big 12 Challenge

The second annual flagship Challenge takes center stage while Big Ten and SEC teams play Group of Six opponents.

Week 4
Rotating cross-conference Challenges

In 2026: Big Ten–Big 12 and SEC–ACC. In 2027: Big Ten–ACC and SEC–Big 12.

Week 5
Second open week

Major-conference teams can use a bye or final scheduled nonconference date. Group of Six conferences begin league play.

Weeks 6–14
Conference season

Major conferences play eight league games with one bye across nine weeks. Group of Six conferences stage championship games in Week 14.

Week 15
CFB Challenge Play-In Weekend

Major-conference cross-division qualification games fill the weekend. The six Group of Six champions play three games, sending three winners to the playoff.

Week 16
National first round

Seeds 9–24 begin the bracket in eight campus games hosted by the higher seeds.

Week 17
National second round

The top eight seeds enter and host the winners from the opening round.

Week 18 · New Year's Day
National quarterfinals

Four games preserve New Year's Day as college football's signature postseason destination.

Week 19
National semifinals

The four surviving teams advance without an unnecessary multiweek layoff.

Week 20
National championship

The season ends with a Saturday night title game and a champion that survived a transparent path.

09

The complete system

Meaningful games are not extra inventory. They are the product.

The CFB Challenge accepts the reality that television wants a larger playoff and more premium events—but ties that inventory to what is best for the sport: current blockbuster matchups, restored conference races, objective rankings and a postseason in which 17 teams earn access by winning on the field.
