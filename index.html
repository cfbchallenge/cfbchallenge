A new way forward for college football

Make the regular season matter more. Not less.

College football doesn't need fewer meaningful games. It needs more. The CFB Challenge is a proposal to turn the Big Ten–SEC rivalry into a nationally televised competition, reward ambitious scheduling, and build a clearer road to the College Football Playoff.

The big picture

Two powerhouse conferences. One national challenge.

Imagine a weekend when the Big Ten and SEC go head-to-head in 16 carefully selected matchups, with conference bragging rights, meaningful national implications, and the entire college football world watching.

Instead of waiting for a playoff matchup to see college football's biggest brands face off, the CFB Challenge would make these games a centerpiece of the regular season.

16 Head-to-Head Games

Sixteen Big Ten teams face sixteen SEC teams in a coordinated challenge designed to deliver compelling games across both conferences.

Commissioners' Classic

A featured showdown serves as the deciding tiebreaker if the conferences split the Challenge 8–8.

A Live Commissioner Draft

The matchups are selected through a strategic draft, creating an event worth watching before a single game is played.

More Meaningful Football

Major nonconference matchups become opportunities to improve a team's playoff position, not unnecessary risks to avoid.

The postseason

A bigger playoff shouldn't mean a smaller regular season.

A 24-team College Football Playoff creates more opportunities to compete for a national championship. But expanding access shouldn't come at the expense of making September, October, and November matter.

The CFB Challenge proposal is built around protecting the value of the regular season through marquee nonconference matchups, transparent rankings, and meaningful rewards for teams that challenge themselves.

More playoff teams. More meaningful games. More reasons to win.

A new approach to rankings

No committees. No eye tests. Earn your ranking.

What if playoff rankings were determined by a transparent, season-long points system instead of a selection committee?

Teams would accumulate points for victories, game control, the location of their games, and the strength of their opponents. The rankings would grow throughout the season, with previous results reassessed as opponents prove themselves.

  • Winning matters most. Beating great teams produces the biggest rewards.
  • Challenge yourself. Competitive losses to elite opponents can still earn valuable points.
  • Performance matters. Game control is determined by the score throughout the game, not subjective impressions.
  • Your résumé evolves. Past wins gain or lose value as opponents improve or struggle.
  • The standings decide. A published formula replaces subjective playoff rankings.

Win games. Challenge yourself. Play well. Keep climbing.

The draft

The matchups are part of the show.

A commissioner draft adds strategy, anticipation, and a new layer of conference competition to the CFB Challenge.

Rather than assigning matchups behind closed doors, commissioners would select opponents in a public draft, weighing team strength, home-field advantage, competitive balance, and the opportunity to create must-watch games.
