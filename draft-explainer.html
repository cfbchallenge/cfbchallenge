The flagship spring event of the CFB Challenge

What if Selection Sunday and the NFL Draft had a college football offseason baby?

That's the Big Ten–SEC Challenge Draft. Every spring, the commissioners build the premier nonconference schedule in college football during a nationally televised event featuring head coaches and returning star players.

16 Challenge matchups created live
Challenge Draft Day

A schedule reveal, selection show and joint media day.

Shortly after spring practice concludes, every participating Big Ten and SEC school sends its head coach and one returning star player to Challenge Draft Day.

As each matchup is announced, both schools take the stage for photographs, interviews and national television coverage. Commissioner strategy becomes part of the show, and every selection immediately creates a season's worth of storylines.

Premium spring television

One major offseason event generates national interest when the college football calendar is otherwise relatively quiet.

Sixteen fall showcases

One live draft creates an entire holiday weekend of marquee football, with meaningful postseason consequences.

Behind the Draft

How the Big Ten–SEC Challenge is built.

Before the draft begins, each conference designates its Standard Bearer. Those programs are paired in the SEC/B1G Commissioners' Classic, the featured matchup of Challenge Weekend, played on Labor Day night.

The Commissioners' Classic is the showcase event regardless of the conference standings. If the Challenge finishes 8–8, its winner also claims the Challenge Championship as the official tiebreaker.

With the Classic established, commissioners draft the remaining fifteen matchups.

Selection 1
Big Ten
The Big Ten selects the first matchup, played on the selected Big Ten team's field.
Selection 2
SEC
The SEC selects the next matchup, played on the selected SEC team's field.
Selections 3–7
Alternating
The commissioners alternate through the first seven drafted matchups.
Selections 8 & 9
SEC, then SEC
The order swaps at the midpoint, giving the SEC both the eighth and ninth selections.
Selections 10–15
Alternating
The conferences alternate the remaining selections until all fifteen drafted matchups are assigned.
The selecting conference controls the home team and the matchup attached to that home date. Each commissioner must balance conference victories, television appeal, rivalry potential, geography and competitive risk.

Why the format works

Build matchups around today's college football.

Traditional nonconference games are often scheduled so far in advance that coaches, rosters and national storylines have changed dramatically by kickoff.

A spring draft reflects the programs and players fans will actually see that fall.

Better Television

Meaningful Results

Stronger Scheduling Incentives

Under the proposed rankings framework, winning major nonconference games produces significant rewards, while competitive losses can still earn credit.

The postseason stakes

The Challenge has real consequences.

Under the CFB Challenge postseason proposal, the winning conference receives five automatic Play-In berths, while the losing conference receives four.

That means a road upset by a middle-tier program can matter just as much to the Challenge standings as a heavyweight matchup in prime time.

Conference-wide success creates conference-wide opportunity. Every participating program has a reason to care about every result.

The individual games also contribute to each team's objective national ranking, making ambitious scheduling more rewarding than simply protecting an undefeated record.

The membership imbalance

What about the other two Big Ten teams?

Because the Big Ten has two more members than the SEC, two Big Ten programs each season will not receive a Big Ten–SEC Challenge matchup.

Those schools instead host regional Group of Five opponents during Challenge Weekend. These games do not count toward the official Big Ten–SEC Challenge standings, but complete each institution's Labor Day weekend schedule.

To avoid financially disadvantaging those programs, the Big Ten Conference funds the game guarantee for each participating Group of Five opponent.
